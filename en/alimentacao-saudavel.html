Healthy Eating

"On the table, on the plate, into the body"

Donate nowLearn more

About the project

Theme

Healthy eating

Subtheme

On the table, on the plate, into the body

Duration

The whole school year

Recipients

Children aged 0 to 5 years and 11 months

Why this project?

Motivation

Meals offered in schools and nursery schools, according to the BNCC, must be healthy and balanced, containing a variety of foods from different food groups. This will have a positive impact on children's health and wellbeing, helping them develop healthy habits and be more active and healthy.

Promoting healthy eating habits for very young children will give them a much better chance of becoming healthy adults, understanding the benefits of a balanced diet and choosing nutritious foods from an early age.

Besides learning to eat well, they will have better school results, since adequate nutrients are important for brain development and children's mental health. It is also an opportunity for inclusion and cultural diversity, as it offers children the chance to try new foods and learn about the different culinary traditions of their culture.

For this reason, through this food project, we will be able to help our children build healthy habits, prevent illness, achieve good school results and learn about the different culinary traditions of their community, through playful, investigative activities.

General objective

Ensure children's rights to play, participate, explore, learn, express themselves and live together, through contact with natural foods, their relationships and transformations, valuing children's leading role, their interests and their individual and collective interactions. Encourage healthy eating, the promotion of good eating habits, disease prevention and knowledge of the different culinary traditions of their cultural community. In an appealing, playful and educational way.

Specific objectives

Encourage knowledge of food groups and their nutritional functions, as well as the foods within each group

Understand the importance of a balanced diet and hydration for health and wellbeing

Learn to prepare simple meals in a healthy, playful and safe way

Encourage autonomy in choosing healthy foods and in personal hygiene at mealtimes

Encourage trying new foods, including foods from other cultures and traditions

Spark children's interest in caring for plants

Raise awareness of the importance of growing food and eating it

Understand the importance of a balanced, healthy diet

Encourage the importance of care and cultivation, following the planting and germination of different plants, legumes and vegetables

Encourage knowledge and tasting of foods of indigenous and African origin

Content by bimester

The project runs throughout the school year, with specific themes for each two-month period.

Bimester 1

February / March

Healthy eating and hydration

Bimester 2

April / May

Vegetables, greens, fruit, grains, legumes and tubers

Bimester 3

June / July

Animal and plant protein sources

Bimester 4

August / September

Salt, sugar and fats

Bimester 5

October / November

Making full use of food

Work strategies

How we organise and carry out the project in the daily life of the nursery school.

Research and explore the topic in depth

Align the objectives, skills and content of the bimonthly plan with the project

Organise activities involving families and children

Plan activities based on observations of children's interests

Organise settings, environments and spaces suited to carrying out the activities

Look for stories based on children's interests and related to the themes

Observe the children, both as a group and in small groups

Record the observations

Look for videos about food to engage the children

Organise and select materials for carrying out the activities

Evaluate the proposed activities, journeys, learning and discoveries of the children and the teacher

Use music to encourage the activities

Evaluation

Complete the report with the activities carried out in the project, to be sent to the PAS every two months.
